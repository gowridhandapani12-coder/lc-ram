Chapter 2 · Reflection of Light · 2.5

Behind the glass,
nothing is there.
Your brain puts it there.

Your reflection looks like a real person standing behind the mirror, but no light ever reaches that spot. Learn how a plane mirror forms an image, why it's reversed left to right, and how to solve problems about distances, speeds, rotating mirrors and full-length mirrors.

    Interactive

    Labs

    Trace rays, flip words, walk to the mirror, rotate it, size a full-length mirror and build a periscope.

    Ray tracer

    Drag the object point P. Rays leave it, reflect off the mirror (angle in = angle out), and the dashed lines show where they appear to come from: the image Q.

    Show
    1Move P further from the mirror. What happens to Q?
    2Is the line PQ always perpendicular to the mirror?
    3Do any real rays reach Q? What makes it a virtual image?
    Mirror writing

    Type a word and see it in a plane mirror. Which letters look the same? Why is AMBULANCE written backwards on the front of ambulances?

    Try
    1Find a word that looks exactly the same in the mirror.
    2Raise your right hand in a real mirror. Which hand does your image raise?
    3Is the image upside down? Why not?
    Collect & fit

    Stand at different distances from a mirror and record how far you are from your image. Plot the data and fit a line: what's the rule?

    Data
    You → mirror (m)You → image (m)

    1Record five distances. What's the gradient of the line?
    2Walk towards the mirror at 1.2 m/s. How fast do you approach your image?
    3Why does the line go through the origin?
    Rotate the mirror

    Keep the incoming ray fixed and turn the mirror. Watch how far the reflected ray turns compared with the mirror.

    1Rotate the mirror by 5°, then 10°, then 20°. Spot the pattern.
    2Does the starting angle of incidence change the rule?
    3Why is this useful for measuring tiny rotations?
    Full-length mirror

    How tall must a wall mirror be for you to see all of yourself? Change your height, eye level and distance and watch the rays from your head and feet.

    1Step back. Does the mirror need to be longer?
    2Compare the mirror length with your height. What's the rule?
    3Where should the bottom of the mirror be?
    Periscope

    Two plane mirrors let you see over a wall. Tilt each mirror and follow the ray: at what angle does the light reach your eye?

    1Set both mirrors to 45°. Which way does the light leave?
    2Change one mirror to 40°. What goes wrong?
    3Is the final image upright? Is it laterally inverted?

    Top exam tips for plane mirrors

    1. Ray diagrams: arrows on every ray, real rays solid, extensions behind the mirror dashed.
    2. Draw the normal at each point of reflection and make angle i = angle r.
    3. Use at least two rays to locate the image where the dashed lines meet.
    4. List the properties: virtual, upright, same size, laterally inverted, same distance behind.
    5. Distances: the image is d behind the mirror, so you to your image is 2d.
    6. Moving at v towards the mirror, you approach your image at 2v.
    7. Rotating a mirror by θ turns the reflected ray by 2θ.
    8. A full-length mirror needs to be half your height. Questions here are practice questions, not official SEC papers.
