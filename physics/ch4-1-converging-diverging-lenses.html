    Parallel beam

    A parallel beam hits a lens. A converging lens brings the rays to the principal focus. A diverging lens spreads them out so they seem to come from a focus behind it.

    Lens
    1Converging: where do the rays cross? Measure it against the focal length.
    2Diverging: follow the dashed lines back. Where do they meet?
    3Make the focal length shorter. Does the lens look fatter or thinner?
    Three special rays

    Rays from the top of an object. Switch each special ray on and see the rule it follows through the lens.

    Lens
    Rays

    2Converging: the parallel ray goes through F. Diverging: where does it seem to come from?

    Collect & fit

    Make lenses with different surface curvatures (radius R) and measure the focal length of each. Plot f against R. A more curved lens (smaller R) has a shorter focal length. (Each measurement has a small random error.)

    Reading test

    Hold a lens over some text and move it away. A converging lens magnifies close up and then flips the image. A diverging lens always makes the text smaller and upright.

    Lens
    1Converging (f = 10 cm): what happens to the letter between 1 cm and 9 cm?
    2Move past 10 cm. What changes?
    3Diverging: can you ever make the letter bigger?
    Why it bends

    A lens bends light by refraction at both surfaces. The further from the centre a ray hits, the more steeply the surfaces slope, so the more it bends. Move the ray up and down.

    Lens
    1Set height 0. Does the ray bend?
    2Double the height. What happens to the angle it bends through?

    Top exam tips for lenses

    1. Converging (convex) = thicker in the middle. Diverging (concave) = thinner in the middle.
    2. Define the principal focus carefully: the point parallel rays converge to, or appear to diverge from, after passing through the lens.
    3. The focal length is the distance from the optical centre to the principal focus.
    4. Draw lenses as the standard symbols (a line with outward or inward arrowheads) or as a clear shape.
    5. Learn the special rays: parallel → through F; through the optical centre → straight on; through F → parallel.
    6. For a diverging lens, draw the dashed back-extensions to the virtual focus.
    7. A diverging lens has a negative focal length in the formula (see 4.5).
    8. The questions here are practice questions, not official SEC papers.
