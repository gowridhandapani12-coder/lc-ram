    The equations

    Set the initial velocity, the acceleration and the time. The car's position is marked every second, and all four equations are checked live.

    1With a = 0, how do the gaps between the marks compare?
    2With a > 0, by how much does each gap grow?
    3Make a negative. When does the car stop?
    Collect & fit

    A trolley rolls down a ramp with an unknown starting velocity and acceleration. Measure its velocity at different times (small error). Fit a line: the gradient is a, the intercept is u, and the area under the line is the distance.

    Data
    t (s)v (m/s)

    1Take six readings. Read u and a from the line.
    2Use the area of the trapezium to find the distance in the first 8 s.
    3Check your distance with s = ut + ½at².
    Which equation?

    Pick the quantity you want and the one that isn't involved. Use the equation that leaves out the uninvolved letter. Example: a car with u = 4 m/s, a = 2 m/s², t = 5 s (so v = 14 m/s, s = 45 m).

    Find
    Not involved
    1Find v without using s. Which equation?
    2Find t without using a. Rearrange it yourself first.
    3Find a without using t. Check the answer is 2.
    Two equations, two unknowns

    A particle passes A, B and C in a straight line with uniform acceleration. It takes t₁ to go from A to B and t₂ from B to C. Each fact gives a line in the (u, a) plane; they cross at the answer.

    1Write the two equations from the readout. Solve them by hand.
    2Make |AB| = |BC| with equal times. What is a?
    3When are the lines parallel?
    Speed up, slow down

    A train starts from rest, accelerates at a₁, then decelerates at a₂ to stop, taking T seconds in all. The v–t graph is a triangle.

    1Check vmax = a₁a₂T ÷ (a₁ + a₂).
    2Make a₁ = a₂. Where is the peak?
    3How does the distance compare with vmax × T?
    Catch up

    A speeding car passes a parked police car at constant speed. The police car starts from rest (after a delay) with constant acceleration. Where the distance–time graphs cross, the police catch up.

    1With no delay, check t = 2v ÷ a.
    2At the moment they meet, how fast is the police car going?
    3When is the gap between them biggest?
    Thrown upwards

    A ball is thrown straight up with speed u. Take up as positive, so a = −9.8 m/s². It passes any height below the top twice: once going up, once coming down.

    1Check: time to the top = u ÷ g, maximum height = u² ÷ 2g.
    2What is the velocity at the top? And the acceleration?
    3Is the time going up equal to the time coming down?
    Test a general result

    Results found with letters work for every number. Pick a result and some values: the lab compares the formula with a step-by-step simulation of the motion (0.001 s steps).

    Result
    1Derive each result yourself from the suvat equations.
    2Try extreme values. Does the formula still match?
    3Why is a letter answer more useful than a number?

    Top exam tips for uniform acceleration

    1. List u, v, a, s, t for each stage of the motion. Circle what you want.
    2. Pick the equation that leaves out the letter you don't know and don't want.
    3. Choose a positive direction and stick to it; deceleration is a negative a.
    4. Sketch the v–t graph: gradient = acceleration, area = distance.
    5. For points A, B, C, write two equations from A (A to B and A to C) and solve simultaneously.
    6. Two objects: write s for each in terms of the same t, then set them equal.
    7. Under gravity, a = −9.8 m/s² if up is positive. At the top, v = 0.
