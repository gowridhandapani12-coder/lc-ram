Projectiles.
Across and up,
handled separately.

A ball, a javelin, a jet of water: once launched, only gravity acts. Split the motion into i (steady speed) and j (uniform acceleration −g), and every projectile question becomes two Chapter 2 problems.

    Labs

    One lab for each section. g = 9.8 m/s²; no air resistance.

    Dropped vs thrown

    From the same height, one ball is dropped and one is thrown horizontally, at the same moment. Their positions are marked every 0.2 s. They are always at the same height: the horizontal speed makes no difference to the fall.

    1Double the horizontal speed. Does the landing time change?
    2How does the distance travelled sideways change?
    3Check the landing time with h = ½gt².
    Up meets down

    Ball A is thrown straight up from the ground at speed u. At the same moment ball B is dropped from height H directly above. Where and when do they meet?

    1Show that they meet at t = H ÷ u.
    2Is A going up or coming down when they meet?
    3Make H large and u small. What goes wrong?
    Fired at an angle

    Launch at speed u and angle α. Slide the time to follow the projectile: its position vector r and velocity vector v, in i and j.

    1Which velocity component never changes?
    2Where is the speed smallest? What is it there?
    3Find the time when the j component of v is zero.
    Collect & fit

    Fix the launch angle and fire at different speeds, measuring the range each time (small error). Theory says R = u² sin 2α ÷ g, so R against u² should be a straight line with gradient sin 2α ÷ g.

    Data
    αu (m/s)u² (m²/s²)R (m)

    1Record five speeds at 30°. Compare the gradient with sin 60° ÷ 9.8.
    2Use the gradient to estimate g.
    3Doubling u multiplies the range by how much?
    Best angle

    For a fixed launch speed, the range depends on the angle: R = u² sin 2α ÷ g. It is biggest at 45°, and angles that add to 90° give the same range. Set a target to see the two angles that hit it.

    1Compare the ranges at 30° and 60°.
    2What is the maximum range in terms of u and g?
    3Move the target beyond u² ÷ g. Why can't you hit it?

    Top exam tips for projectiles

    1. Write the initial velocity in i and j: u cos α i + u sin α j.
    2. i direction: no acceleration, so x = (u cos α)t.
    3. j direction: a = −g, so y = (u sin α)t − ½gt² and vj = u sin α − gt.
    4. Lands back at the same level: y = 0. Highest point: vj = 0.
    5. Time is the link between the two directions: find t in one, use it in the other.
    6. Derive range and time of flight from the equations; don't just quote them unless allowed.
    7. Speed at any moment = √(vi² + vj²); direction = tan⁻¹(vj ÷ vi).
