Chapter 2 · Reflection of Light · 2.4

Two rules.
Every mirror.
Angle in = angle out.

Every reflection, from a bathroom mirror to a laser bouncing off the Moon, follows the same two laws. Learn the names of the rays and angles, prove the laws with a ray box, and use them to aim light exactly where you want it.

    Collect & fit

    The classic experiment: shine a ray box at a mirror, mark the rays, draw the normal and measure i and r with a protractor. Each reading has a small measuring error. Plot r against i.

    Run it backwards

    Light goes from A to B by reflecting off a mirror. Reverse it: light from B follows exactly the same path back to A.

    Light starts at
    1Switch between A and B. Does the path change?
    2Which angle was the angle of incidence before, and which is it now?
    3If you can see someone's eyes in a mirror, can they see yours?
    Two mirrors

    Two mirrors meet at a corner. A ray reflects off one and then the other. At 90° the ray comes straight back the way it came, parallel to itself.

    1At 90°, change the first angle. Does the ray still come back parallel?
    2Check: angle of incidence at mirror 2 = (angle between mirrors) − (first angle).
    3Why are bicycle reflectors made of tiny 90° corners?
    Hit the target

    A lamp shines on a mirror you can tilt. Use the law of reflection to turn the mirror so the reflected ray hits the target.

    Target
    1Hit three targets. Which way do you turn the mirror to move the ray up?
    2Turn the mirror by 5°. How far does the reflected ray turn?
    3When you hit the target, does i still equal r?

    Top exam tips for the laws of reflection

    1. Learn both laws word for word: same plane; angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
    2. Angles are measured from the normal, never from the mirror.
    3. If a question gives the angle to the mirror, subtract it from 90° first.
    4. The angle between the incident and reflected rays is 2i.
    5. In the experiment, draw the normal with a protractor or set square at 90° to the mirror line.
    6. Repeat for at least five angles and show r = i each time (or plot r against i: a straight line through the origin, gradient 1).
    7. Every ray diagram needs arrows on the rays and a dashed normal.
    8. The questions here are practice questions, not official SEC papers.
